Two things to know

Keep this page open and the screen on while you record. If Safari goes to the background or the screen locks, the recording stops. The app tries to keep the screen awake for you.

Your phone could not keep the screen awake automatically — tap the screen now and then so it does not lock during a take.

Listen when you tap record. If the beat suddenly sounds muddy and flat, your headphones dropped to phone-call mode and the recording will sound bad. Stop the take, take the headphones off, switch to “No headphones” below, and record with the phone speaker instead.

Setup

Headphones

With the phone speaker there is nothing to measure — the timing is handled from the phone’s own numbers, and the check after each take tidies up what’s left.

Wireless headphones delay the sound a little. Measure yours once, and the beat will play early by exactly that much — so you hear it on time and your voice lands in time.

Put the headphones next to the phone — not on your ears — turn the volume up, and keep them still. The app plays 5 clicks and times how long they take to come back.

Headphones not measured yet

Your headphones aren’t measured yet, so the timing won’t be as tight on this take. Measure them first — it takes about 10 seconds — or record anyway.

Sound check

Check my mic

Records 10 seconds with no beat, so Jim can check which mic your phone used. If the phone mic is doing the work, the recording sounds full and clear. If the headphones’ mic took over, it sounds thin and muffled — and Jim will tell you.

Your takes stay on this phone until you send them. The app works offline after the first load — add it to your home screen from the share menu in Safari.

How it works, in one line: the app measures your headphones’ delay once, plays the beat early by that much while you sing, then checks the timing after every take and lines it up.
